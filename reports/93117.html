我醒来时想到的第一件事就是我今天的饮料. 没有它, 我会感到疲倦, 易怒, 疼痛. 这听起来好像我在谈论某种强烈的药物. 我说的是世界上最广泛的饮料之一 - - 咖啡. 自从高中以来, 这一直是个不停的斗争. 我从小就开始每天喝一杯, 每天早上喝两杯,有时三杯, 我在参加越野运动队后, 终于放弃了. 如果我每走5英里都必须停下来尿尿, 两三天后就消失了. 没有问题. 秋天到来时, 跑步季结束了, 空气清新, 叶子在落下, 我决定庆祝我最喜欢的季节的到来, 我变得过度活跃, 像紧张的未来父亲一样抽和步行, 咖啡不再是一种乐趣, 夏季假期是一件糟糕的事情. 经济已经崩, 留下我在家, 没有工作, 没有汽油. 现在回想起来, 我很喜欢它; 无论如何, 我每天喝一杯咖啡, 再加上至少四汽水. 我已经超越了. 咖啡因抑制食欲的特性导致了有趣的饮食. 我早上九点左右醒来,倒了咖啡. 我通常在吃早餐之前喝两三杯, 我吃了一袋爆米花. 这就是午餐. 晚上我吃了大部分的晚餐, 对于一个已经体重不足的青少年来说, 咖啡已经证明是危险的. 夏天的结束已经到来. 我发现自己再次报名参加越野队. 通过将无咖啡因咖啡与普通咖啡混合, 我一天只能喝一杯. 这是一个恶性循环, 在我四年高中期间, 一天只能喝一杯酒, 夏季假期, 越野赛季每天只能喝一杯, 赛季结束后, 每天只能喝三杯, 夏季假期到来后, 再一次喝一杯. 我第一次真正的咖啡因戒断是高中初年后的夏天. 我父母非常虔诚, 强迫我和兄弟们去教堂. 他们也喜欢,但我不相信右翼的宣传. 无论如何, 他们还让我去参加一个教会夏令营, 一群轻信的孩子会因为他们是罪人而哭泣, 发誓要为上帝活下去, 无论如何, 在这些愚蠢的营地里, 但这一次发生在我们的青年牧师离开教堂后. 一些拙的白痴从教会管理层接管并把它变成一个完整的集体. 我们骑马了一小时,然后乘小艇短暂出游, 剩下的时间坐在烂的床上, 偷偷看偷偷来的电视节目. 最重要的是,没有咖啡. 很快,我发现自己有点. 到了晚上, 我的头痛变成了令人头痛的偏头痛. 我甚至无法与人互动. 我的身体感觉像是制成的. 我很模糊地记得我在床上昏倒了几个小时, 我认为要去医院. 随着时间的推移, 到了第四天,当我们都乘坐了回家的巴士时, 我感觉几乎正常了. 只有当我拿到咖啡机的时候, 我回到一个的一天. 快速推进几年. 我毕业高中,结婚 (我知道我还年轻), 这是一个长故事. 我很快就让她怀孕了, 于是我意识到我也许应该为这个依赖问题做点什么. 解决方案很简单 - - 我允许自己每天只一. 我每天的饮用量降至两杯. 九个月后,我的儿子出生了. 让我告诉你一件事, 新生儿是另一个东西. 忘了睡觉. 忘记吃饭或使用浴室, 我现在才开始习惯这种完全失控的感觉. 但最困难的部分是失眠. 我儿子的第一个月, 昼夜混在一起, 尽管他的白天和夜晚被交换了, 他仍然没有睡觉. 他曾经是, 现在仍然是, 一个非常挑的, 需要很高的婴儿, 这是一个非常艰难,但非常有价值的经历. 无论如何,我买了一个更大的咖啡. 现在我的儿子已经14个月大了, 睡眠也好了, 当然,我已经努力戒烟. 很久以前我用茶代替了咖啡, 但压力让我重新开始喝咖啡. 我毫不怀疑我的身体化学是可能永久地改变了我不断的放纵. 我相信黑酒将永远存在, 呼唤我的名字, 这不是一个选择,这是一个需要. 一个需要不会消失,直到他们扔灰尘在盒子里我永久居住. 咖啡是很好的适度. 但最轻微的过度放纵会导致成,在我看来, 时间表: 93117 咖啡因 (11):一般性 (1),回顾/总结 (11),成和习惯性 (10),不适用 (38)

Coffee Slave Caffeine by hooked Citation:   hooked. "Coffee Slave: An Experience with Caffeine (exp93117)". Erowid.org . Nov 2, 2012. erowid.org/exp/93117 DOSE: 3 cups oral Caffeine (daily) BODY WEIGHT: 150 lb I am an addict. The first thought that crosses my mind upon waking up is getting my fix for the day. Without it, I am tired, irritable, and achy. It probably sounds like I'm talking about some kind of hard drug. But no, I'm talking about one of the most widely consumed beverages in the world -- coffee. It's been an on-and-off struggle since high school. I started having a cup a day when I was younger, just to help get my brain kick-started. With the newfound freedom and stress of high school, I saw no problem with drinking two, sometimes three cups every morning. I managed to quit after I signed up for the cross country team -- no sense in running five miles if I have to stop and piss every step along the way. I experienced a mild headache and some heart palpitations, which disappeared after two or three days. No problem. Autumn arrived when the running season ended -- the air was crisp, the leaves were falling, and halloween decorations began appearing around the neighborhood. I decided to celebrate the arrival of my favorite season by kicking back some coffee -- first one cup, then two, then three. I became hyperactive, twitching and pacing like a nervous father-to-be, but after a few days of consumption the effects mellowed out. Coffee ceased to be fun; it had become a need once again. Summer vacation was a bummer. The economy had died and gone to hell, leaving me stuck home with no job and no gas in the car. In retrospect I enjoyed it; I was a stay-at-home kinda guy anyway, and I could just spend my whole day playing on the computer if I wanted to. But boredom is a dangerous thing -- I was drinking a whole pot of coffee a day, plus at least four cans of soda. I was beyond hooked. Caffeine's appetite suppressing qualities resulted in an interesting diet. I woke up around nine in the morning and poured my coffee. I usually consumed two or three cups before I got around to eating breakfast, which consisted of one or two cheese sticks. At about two in the afternoon, I had a bag of popcorn. That was lunch. I managed to eat most of my dinner in the evening, but only because my mom would get worried if I didn't. For a teenager who was already underweight, coffee was proving to be dangerous. The end of summer rolled around. I found myself signing up for the cross country team again. I managed to gradually reduce my consumption by mixing decaf with regular coffee. I was down to a cup a day in no time. This was a vicious cycle that repeated itself for the four years that I was in high school -- an entire pot per day during summer vacation, no more than a cup a day during cross country season, three cups a day when the season ended, and an entire pot once again when summer vacation rolled around. It was during the summer after my junior year of high school that I had my first real caffeine withdrawal. My parents were very religious, forcing my brothers and I to go to church. Well, they were into it, but I wasn't buying the right-wing propaganda. Anyway, they also made me go to a church camp in the summer, which was one of the most colossal wastes of time in my life. A bunch of gullible kids would cry because they were sinners, vow to live their lives for god, go home and try to convert their friends so that they wouldn't be cast into hell by this god, and then forget about all of it in a week. At any rate, I usually had access to coffee during these stupid camps. But this particular one occurred after our youth pastor had left the church. Some bumbling idiot from the church administration took over and turned it into a complete clusterfuck. We rode horses for an hour, went on a brief canoe trip, and spent the rest of the camp sitting on our moldy beds watching shows on a smuggled television. To top it all off, there was no coffee. The morning of the first day, I was irritable and had a mild headache. By that evening, my headache had morphed into a head-splitting migraine of epic proportions. I couldn't even interact with people. My body felt like it was made of gelatin. I vaguely remember passing out on my bed for a few hours but had no idea how I got there or how I fell asleep. The heart palpitations were so bad that I thought about going to the hospital. This hellish existence became more tolerable as the days went on. By the fourth day, when we all boarded that bus to go home, I was feeling almost normal. That all ended once I got my hands on the coffee maker, of course. I was back to a pot a day. Fast forward a few years. I graduated high school, got married (young, I know), and had moved over 1,000 miles away to be in Maine with my wife. It's a long story. At any rate, I promptly got her pregnant, at which point I realized that maybe I should be doing something about this dependency problem. The solution was simple -- buy a smaller coffee pot. It could only contain a small amount at a time and I permitted myself to make only one pot a day. My consumption fell to two cups a day. Nine months later, my son was born. Let me tell you something, newborns are something else. Forget sleep. Forget eating or using the bathroom when you want to. It was a complete loss of control that I'm just now getting accustomed to. But the hardest part was losing sleep. For the first month, my son had night and day mixed up, meaning my wife and I would take turns pulling all-nighters. Even after he got his days and nights switched around, he simply did not sleep. He was, and still is, a very fussy, high-needs baby who has trouble sleeping. It's been a very hard, but very rewarding experience. At any rate, I bought a bigger coffee pot. My son is now fourteen months old and sleeping better, but I'm still going through a whole pot every morning and can't foresee myself quitting any time soon. Sure, I've made efforts to quit. Not too long ago I replaced coffee with tea and managed to do quite well for a while. But stress got the best of me and I went back to coffee. I have no doubt that my body chemistry is perhaps permanently altered by my constant indulgence. I believe that the black brew will always be there, calling my name, beckoning me to partake of the bitter water that has become the cornerstone of my life. It's not a choice, it's a need. A need that won't disappear until they are throwing dirt over the box in which I permanently reside. Coffee is great in moderation. But the slightest over-indulgence will lead to addiction that, in my opinion, is stronger than cocaine.
