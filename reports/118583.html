Soothing the Restless Mind DMXE by JohnK Citation:   JohnK. "Soothing the Restless Mind: An Experience with DMXE (exp118583)". Erowid.org . Dec 30, 2024. erowid.org/exp/118583 DOSE: T+ 0:00 10 mg insufflated DMXE (ground / crushed) T+ 0:30 10 mg insufflated DMXE (ground / crushed) T+ 3:00 0.5 mg oral Melatonin BODY WEIGHT: 80 kg I’ve been self-medicating for about 25 years to manage what I later discovered in my late 30s was undiagnosed ADHD. The most effective treatment for me has always been full-psychedelic doses of psilocybin mushrooms. A long walk alone in nature with a dramatic ego-death experience seems to "defragment" my mind and keeps me stable for at least six months. However, as I’ve gotten older, it takes a lot more effort to psych myself up for these trips. While they’re amazing, they can also be quite exhausting. So, I started looking for other options to help fill the gaps between those trips, especially for moments of crisis—like during the winter when my thoughts tend to spiral into dark places. I’ve read a lot of scientific papers about mind-altering substances, and ketamine caught my attention. Unfortunately, it’s hard to find trustworthy sources for it. That’s when I came across deoxymethoxetamine (DMXE), a novel ketamine analogue with a slightly longer duration of action. There isn’t much research on it yet—just a few papers mostly focusing on its NMDA receptor affinity. One study showing disturbing brain scans of chronic ketamine users reminded me to proceed with caution. I also read all the user reports I could find before trying it myself. For my trial, I bought two gray pellets from a Dutch manufacturer, each containing 40 mg of DMXE. The Trial After a week of reading and preparation, I decided to carefully try a dissociative for the first time. I figured a quarter of a pellet (10 mg) taken intranasally would be a reasonable starting point—unlikely to harm my brain or be dangerous. I crushed a piece of the pill and sniffed it in one go. No burning, just a slight pharmaceutical sting and a faint smell/flavor that reminded me a bit of cocaine. +5 minutes: I feel something happening—a subtle numbness in my nose. +15 minutes: I’m feeling tipsy but calm. I keep observing myself, but there are no signs of any physical or mental discomfort. +30 minutes: I feel so relaxed and at peace. I decide to add another 10 mg. +60 minutes: The second dose kicks in gently, building on the first. The feeling is unlike anything I’ve experienced before. I’m trying to analyze this state but can’t quite pin it down. I open my laptop and start searching for music on YouTube. Typically, I lean towards ambient artists like Tim Hecker, Biosphere, or Boards of Canada when on psychedelics. For some reason, YouTube suggests Led Zeppelin. I’d never been much of a fan, but I decided to give Stairway to Heaven (live) a try. The first notes felt like they were caressing my soul. I melted into the music. Every tone seemed to pull on a string directly attached to a blissful non-existence. It was one of the best musical experiences of my life. I smiled and giggled, completely absorbed, savoring every second of it. When the song ended, I realized the drug’s effects were quite pronounced. I had completely lost myself in the music for a while. Feeling thirsty, I got up to grab some water, but walking felt like I’d had a couple of beers—light-headed but steady. In the kitchen, I kept forgetting why I was there. It was hard to focus on my intentions, but I didn’t feel frustrated about it. Eventually, I managed to drink a glass of water and return to the music. I spent the rest of the evening listening to various genres, astonished and completely absorbed. +2–3 hours: The effects gradually faded, leaving me in a calm, relaxed mood. I felt pleasantly tired but struggled to fall asleep, so I took 0.5 mg of melatonin. That did the trick, and I was asleep within 30 minutes. The Aftermath In the days that followed, I noticed no sign of depression. My usual habit of having two beers to unwind in the evening disappeared. I just felt fine. That said, I did notice a mild craving to try DMXE again—not an overwhelming urge, but I’d be lying if I said I wasn’t tempted. I plan to wait a week or two before revisiting that "zen state." Here’s the most surprising takeaway: DMXE seemed to turn off my "Radio ADHD." The constant inner dialogue, parallel chains of thoughts, fantasies, and overthinking—all of it was gone for a while. It felt almost too good to be true. I’ll definitely proceed with caution because this stuff is powerful.
