Aug 8, 2025. erowid.org/exp/119125 DOSE: T+ 0:00 112.5 mg oral Pharms - Venlafaxine (daily) T+ 2:45 10 mg oral Cannabis - High THC (edible / food) BODY WEIGHT: 112 lb This report is based off my memories as well as the journal entries I wrote at the time. It should also be noted that I take 112.5 mg of venlafaxine at 10 am daily for my depression, which may have affected my experience. This was also only my third time trying cannabis, and my first time at 10mg. I took a 10mg THC gummy at 12:45 pm. I was home alone at the time. Before consumption I ate a bowl of Rice Krispies with milk and two large spoonfuls of peanut butter, as I read that fatty foods are supposed to increase the effects. I recorded at 1:39 pm that I had begun to feel “more smiley” and the back of my head and the inside of my throat had started feeling cool and tingly. I also noted at 1:45 pm that when I closed my eyes and laid down I saw an image of something pulling the skin at the top of my skull into a rectangular prism shape, then swirling around. I was tired prior to taking the edible, and attributed this to what it feels like when I start to slip into deep sleep. However, this occurred in too brief a period for that to be the explanation. I also noted at this time that I was “easily forgetting things ‘cause I get distracted so quickly.” At 2:09 pm I recorded that I closed my eyes again and watched as “flashing images turned into each other”, as in, one image would morph into another. My mind fixated on two images in particular and kept returning to them. One was rounded at the top and bottom and pinched in at the middle, like an hourglass, with round wood patterns on it. Alternately, it had a gold-painted vine pattern on it, but this version stopped appearing. The second image was two flat wooden panels facing each other, with a smooth dark green texture at the top and holes on the ends. I also had my voice in the background of my thoughts, repeating multiple words unrelated to each other. The background words continued when my eyes were open. I attempted to record the words as they came, but they were too fast to write down, and were a bunch of unrelated nonsense anyway. I also continued to see image 1 in my mind’s eye and it evoked certain feelings of childhood, “like a wood puzzle piece or something that goes away like a bookshelf.” I think I was trying to get at the fragility of childhood memories, like having my dad read a picture book to me, but it was all going by too quickly to make any sense. I also realized that if I focused on what was around me the voice went silent. The background voice and images were in my imagination, and didn’t interact with my physical environment at all. At 2:18 pm I began underlining words in my journal that reminded me of the first image, the wooden puzzle piece. I couldn’t make sense of why they were related - it almost felt like a sense of deja vu, a familiarity evoked by the word that linked it to the image somehow. I believed it to be linked to the sounds the words made because I recognized the connection made no sense, and noted “a linguist should study me while I’m high.” I linked the word “study” with image 2, and began to feel that image 2 evoked the feeling of my dad working before I was born. (Sober me realized later that night that image 2 was the same texture of the desks in the university library, the same university my dad attended, so this somehow vaguely makes sense.) I noted, “I can only understand this while it’s fresh in my head, then I’m like what the hell, this doesn’t make sense.” The sense of deja vu faded as quickly as it came. The background voice was absent as I tried to understand image 2, and I only noticed it was gone when it returned. In any case, I described it as “my creative mind going crazy,” as I was drawing associations between feelings and words and objects quicker than I could understand how they were connected. I was drawing associations between feelings and words and objects quicker than I could understand how they were connected. I forgot to record the time, but I also experienced hunger at this time and ate quite a bit - pudding, Mac and cheese, chocolate wafers, and a bowl of soup. As I interacted with things in the kitchen I continued to feel the sense that they were related to images 1 and 2. Somehow, the shape of macaroni was image 1. I didn’t have a better way to describe the sensation than synesthesia, and I still don’t know if that’s the correct term. The background words kept repeating the word “uh-oh” every few words and I thought this was really funny. In general, everything in the show I was watching felt more funny. I felt really happy, like getting up and dancing for no reason. I also noted sensitivity to the heat of my macaroni. At 3:12 pm, I noted that the synesthesia (?) was not happening as frequently anymore, and the background words were absent. This could be because I was watching a show to distract me, however. At 4:40 pm, I noted that the associations/synesthesia I had been making before were gone, but I still felt a pleasant euphoria. This lasted until around 6pm. All in all, it was an enjoyable experience, but I don’t understand how artists on cannabis are able to create something based on their ideas when my mind on cannabis was going much too fast to record all of the ideas and associations in it, and even while high I recognized they made no sense.
