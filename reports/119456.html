Shamanic Awakening Substance: Psilocybe atlantis sclerotium Dose: ~6g Subjective intensity: 2/5 User: 42 M 91kg Responsibility always comes first, and fun thereafter... if time allows. And so it happened once that the mushroom's calling was ignored until the very last opportunity, and then some more. For the last several months the need for a deep emotional experience kept growing, but due to a lack of free time it could not be satisfied before my departure for a two week exploration in the tropics. I wanted to know how I felt about this journey. I needed to know that I could still feel anything. Most of the time I live my life like an automaton; do my job, play with my kids, attend to my hobbies, but all of this is just going through the motions. Why am I doing anything if nothing makes me happy? What's the meaning of my life? What's the point of any activity beyond keeping myself busy, keeping my mind occupied and distracted until I grow old and die? I'm just passing time, waiting for some miracle which never comes. A long time ago I used to feel. I used to have dreams, hopes. Then I only felt loneliness and despair, and finally it seemed better to build up my walls real high, and shut out all emotions. Then came emptiness. Just going through the motions day after day, week after week, year after year. Duties, and more duties; a neverending sequence of to-dos. The final day before my departure came and went. And so it happened, that I wrapped up 8.27g of fresh Psilocybe atlantis truffles in a simple handkerchief, stuffed it into a pocket of my backpack, and smuggled an illegal substance through international airports, albeit only a single dose for personal use, to be taken on the other side of the ocean. That day was long and eventful. The day of the experience, I mean. I met new people, and encountered a level of hospitality like never before, which filled my mind with gratitude and positive energy. I was ready. On the other hand, I was very exhausted and dirty from daylong hiking in heat and humidity. The cheap hotel I stayed at had no air-conditioning, only a noisy ceiling fan. Needless to say, it wasn't enough, so I took off my shirt, pants and socks, leaving only my boxer shorts on for the sake of decency. I still had to prepare my suitcase for an early leave the next morning. Packing, resting, and a trip to psychedelia. Sounded like too much for one night, but my actions were driven by instincts, not by reason. Packing, resting, and a trip to psychedelia. Sounded like too much for one night, but my actions were driven by instincts, not by reason. A few days earlier I already took about 2g, but had to stop because of the horrible taste. On 20th May, 2025 I ate the remaining roughly 6g magic truffles. The 6g went down fast and effortlessly. By this time they have dried out completely under the tropical sun, and shriveled up like wrinkled little raisins. They were hard to chew, but still tasted the same; a chemical sourness which reminds me of walnuts soaked in household bleach. A bit of milk helped wash them down. I started working; first unloading the suitcase completely, and spreading all of my stuff out on the bed, the table, and the floor. Then I looked around reluctantly for a while, not even knowing where to begin. The room was a mess, and the task of cleaning up and putting everything neatly back into the suitcase seemed insurmountable. I glanced at the large mirror on the wall, and the sight wasn't heartwarming. Those boxer shorts did very little to grant me a decent appearance. What stared back at me from the mirror, was a hairy creature in urgent need of a good shave, with head and arms reddish brown from sun exposure, and a body pale thanks to UV blocking fabrics. I looked like a joke; a weird advanced monkey, scorched under the glaring face of the sun, running around in the heat all day chasing meaninglessness tasks, then collapsing into bed with exhaustion, only to start all over again the next morning. Suddenly a dizziness came over me. Great! I have work to do, but instead of being responsible and efficient, I got myself drunk on mushrooms. Exactly what I didn't need. I had to sit down because the room was swirling, and I wondered how this roughly chewed dry fungus could hit so fast. It was only 15-20 minutes since consumption. I still managed to organize and tidy up the room surprisingly fast, though I can't explain or remember how I did it. Task complete; It was time to take a shower before the experience peaked. For those who have no experience with tropical hiking, it has to be explained that one cannot spend several weeks in the same pants, because they would get smelly and turn gray from the sticky sweat and dust, making the tourist look like a homeless person. Every two or three days the pants have to be washed, with soap, while showering. That's a well proven method. T-shirts made of high-tech synthetic fabric can be reused for up to three days if washed every night. Socks... now those have to be changed daily. OK? I don't ever try to spare luggage space so much that I would go on a 16 day trip with less than 16 pairs of socks! They'll be of good use on the way home to prank those drug-sniffing dogs at the airport luggage control. So into the shower I went with my dirty pants. My head was still spinning, and leaving the light switched off felt more comfortable than being in harsh artificial glow. I set the water temperature, moistened the pants, and started rubbing them with the soap bar. Rub, rinse, repeat. Over and over again. Dirt being washed away, going down the drain. Rub, rinse, repeat. Head spinning, mind expanding, listening to the splashing water, alone in the dark in a small cubicle. Rub, rinse, repeat. It felt nice to do this usual task. Something I'm well familiar with. Something that doesn't require much thinking. Rub, rinse, repeat. How long have I been doing this? Time duration became somewhat incomprehensible, and I could not unequivocally tie this thought to a real world notion. The question seemed to take on two parallel meanings simultaneously: How long have I been washing my pants? and How long have I been on this hiking trip, or chasing my hobby altogether? Have I been in the shower for hours? Days?... Years? Is anybody in the outside world looking for me? Do they even care that I disappeared? Alone in the dark in a small cubicle, listening to the splashing water, doing a single repetitive task, and slowly going insane. I had to escape before completely losing track of time or contact with reality. Out of the shower and back to the bedroom. I glanced at the clock, which mercilessly gave me the news that it was +1 hour since dosing. This is the start of the peak, or the storm as I prefer to call it, because oftentimes one has to hold on tight to get through the crazy hour... Suddenly my phone beeped with the notification sound of an incoming message. Usually I switch my phone to airplane mode or turn it off completely when I take mushrooms to prevent myself from reaching out to anybody and potentially raising suspicion by acting in strange ways. It seems this time I forgot this safety measure. I tried to overcome the intoxication as much as I could, and grabbed the phone. The message came from my son. He's at the age where he has just recently learned the concept of communicating through text messages, and while he enjoys chatting, he often just sends emojies or random stuff. I wrote back, then saw the tree dots appear at the bottom, signaling that the person at the other end is typing. They vanished, then reappeared, again and again as if to tease me. Dancing, dancing, three little dots, but no message. I cheered for him with all my heart while rolling on the bed with laughter. "Come on little guy, type! Send me something!" And he did. We had so much fun, father and son chatting about nonsense in short texts. Having children is one of the best parts of life, even if it means that all romance evaporates from the marriage. Never regret anything! I was rolling with laughter, tears in my eyes, connecting with my dear, dear son eight timezones apart. I wondered if there was still anyone in the next room, and if the music would bother them or if they would enjoy listening. These cheap hotels are used by the locals for romantic purposes, and the previous night I heard a couple having loud intercourse on the other side of the thin wall. It turned me on, but at the same time made me feel very lonely and unlovable. And now, in the silence of a rundown hotel possibly with no other guests I felt even lonelier. I wished they would come back for another round. I hoped they would be happy together for a lifetime. The music was melodic and calming, and I turned off the lights in the room as the sense of vision was at this point unnecessary. Standing in the darkness with my face turned towards the ceiling, I was floating weightlessly in a deep ocean, focusing my attention on the trace of energy radiating down on me from the surface. Up there is light and life. Down here is stillness and silence. Peace, safety, balance. A happy existence. While this depth might have felt empty to others, I am highly adapted and at home in this environment. I studied it for a while, observing my mental state almost as an outsider. I understood how the drug was amplifying whatever was in my soul, and therefore my soul had to be balanced, lest any small disturbance could cause an avalanche.
