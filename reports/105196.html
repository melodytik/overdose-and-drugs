Once I got home googled Zen and read a bit about it, and I think I reached what the followers of Zen might call 'enlightenment.' I just 'was.' I was not me, my belongings or desires, I was. The 'I' disappeared. I was in absolute bliss. It was beyond euphoria; I was not distinctly happy, but content. So content, however, that it felt good. I couldn't stop smiling. I will try to explain it in another way: Self implies other. 'I' implies not-I. We use these symbols, represented here by words and in your mind by various concepts which you can feel as 'distinct.' The problem is, as soon as you start dividing things up, you can never stop. Once you divide up meaning and say 'this stuff here is a cat,' you can never describe what the cat REALLY is with 100% accuracy. The following are all true statements about the cat: * It has four legs. * It is not a car. * It cannot fly. * It is composed of atoms, arranged in the following order... (expands to a near-infinite set of statements) * It is NOT composed of horses, or spaceships, or.... (onwards with any set of symbols you can put together and call a noun) See how that goes on forever? You can never actually pinpoint what the cat really is when you divide up meaning. There is always something missing, since any division creates more possibilities. Does the cat have four legs, or three? It has four, not three. What about 5? 500? 1000? Let's say you have 'something.' As soon as you classify part of that something as 'White' and another part as 'Black,' you lose part of the inherent meaning. The very act of dividing that 'something' up into symbols/knowledge/meaning causes it to lose that special quality of wholeness. In the pizza place, my mind was whole. I can now understand why people think of this as a spiritual experience. In non-mathematical non-rational terms, I felt infinite. I was me, the chair I was sitting on, and the phone I was holding. I had transcended myself. The futility of describing this state in language, as someone speaking to you, is very frustrating. I was not experiencing it, IT just WAS. You know how people say you have to just 'be?' You need to BE present, just BE and quiet your mind? Well, I was beyond *being,* I was *happening.* Being requires an action, you have to BE. I was like an effect without a cause. That is as best as I can describe it. And it was amazing. I assume that the only way to reach that state without drugs, is through very deep meditation. --------] The Trip - Part 5 (Comedown) [T+ 7h] After getting home from the pizza place, I ate half of the pizza in complete darkness while thinking about what I had learned. My sense of self was becoming more and more overpowering, so I was trying to process my insights as quickly as possible before losing the ability to detach from my ego. The rest of the night was very pleasant. I more or less sobered up 8 hours later, and spent the next 5 hours in bed reading about Zen, watching Alan Watts videos, and being extremely content and happy. It really is true that if you take away the 'I' and 'want' from 'I want happiness,' you are happy. --------] Conclusion This was extremely interesting to me, since I finally understood the implications of GEB intuitively. I could see the ego distinct from 'something' else, which I can no longer do now that I'm sober. Since I remember the feeling, I know that 'I' am extremely limited and the intuitive understanding of the GEB 'I' problem remains with me. The really funny part is that I never studied Zen, but I understood every Zen koan I read that night after getting home, and still do. So Zen has some truth to it, but 'practicing' Zen completely misses the point. Practicing implies not being, just as trying implies not doing. As soon as you try to explain it, you have missed the point. Which makes this entire writeup very ironic. All in all, it was a really amazing experience. Describing it with symbols instantly loses some of the meaning, so I recommend you experience it for yourself. If you try to, be prepared, and try to keep from puking the seeds up. I think the strength of my trip was due to me keeping the seeds down, and absorbing all of the LSA. It was not, however, 'fun,' but instead extremely insightful. LSA is not a party drug.
